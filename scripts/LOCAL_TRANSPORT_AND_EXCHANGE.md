# Counting returns is not yet an identification of space or particles

The [square-intervention survey](RELATIONAL_OBSERVERS_AND_COVERS.md)
found two inequivalent rank-three laws for records of closed signal paths.
They can be distinguished without assigning three spatial coordinates.
Count distinct free-period records for trips returning to the observer in
at most four unit ticks: the distinguished root gives **13**, whereas every
start in the nonrooted example gives **3, 5 or 7**. Modulo-three counters
still distinguish these cases, but modulo-two counters can alias them.
Neither saturation of a finite counter nor a single count of seven proves
the number of independent periods.

For the rooted rank-three example the minimal unit-hop cost of a period
with coordinates p in a measured loop basis is four times the larger
of its total positive or negative multiplicities. Two basis loops u,v
give costs 4,4,8,4 for u,v,u+v,u−v: these violate the parallelogram
identity required for an inner-product norm. A symmetric local delay
changes them to 5,5,10,6 without changing the rank. The limiting velocity
bodies in the two rank-three models are linearly equivalent, but the fast
four-hop periods generate index one in one lattice and index two in the
other. This result concerns the specified reciprocal route clock, root
recognition and signed period counters, not an emergent Lorentz metric.

## What the carrier does to an exchange path

Give two identical occupations reciprocal hops, disallow a shared site,
and equate the two orders of independent moves on disjoint bonds. Add
records of closed paths commutatively. The 120 unordered pairs give 441
independent graph loops before the 620 commuting-square relations. Their
integer group reduces to 25 free periods and **one order-two period**.
A six-hop exchange on a Y-shaped neighborhood realizes the order-two
class: killing a single lap removes it, identifying a double lap changes
nothing. If single-packet square faces also contract with a spectator
outside them, the group instead consists of two independent binary
periods; the same exchange still has order two. On an isolated Y graph
or a planar tetrahedron the exchange has infinite order. Allowing joint
occupation and the corresponding commuting moves on the full carrier
makes this exchange trivial.

If route records are observed through scalar unit phases, an order-two
class may map to +1 **or** −1. This is a finite example of the mathematical
distinction in [Harrison et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5781).
Neither the hard-core assumption nor choosing a phase character explains
the Pauli principle. Here no spin, spacetime dimension or counting rule
was derived.

Equal real kinetic amplitudes do not select the negative sign. With the
energy operator minus the adjacency, the even sector has the lower ground
energy by √41−3. With plus adjacency the two minima tie at −6; with
ordinary positive springs only the even sector has a zero mode. A short
intervention distinguishes the signs without a ground-state argument:
occupy two leaves of a three-arm junction and probe its center and the
third leaf after three steps. In the isolated Y the two coherent, real
transfer contributions sum to twice the product of the three spoke
weights in the even sector, and to zero in the odd sector. Doubling one
transfer conditional on the spectator can lift that zero. A transfer
amplitude, including a dark output, is not an observed event frequency.

## Measured exchange-sensitive contrasts

[Bocquillon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.7093) measured a dip of
0.45 ± 0.05 (fitted fractional depth) in low-frequency excess electronic
noise at a splitter. They did **not** count individual output pairs.
Electron–hole timing is a useful control on a simple repulsion-only
account. In an independent hot-electron regime,
[Fletcher et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03473) reported roughly
30% peak noise suppression involving Coulomb interactions. In a different
system, [Preiss et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12939) measured a
momentum-fringe contrast of 79(2)% for identical lithium atoms; separations
below 30 μm were unresolved. These are deliberately **not pooled**.

With independent input tags and an externally supplied lossless splitter,
an odd exchange sign lowers the normalized partition noise when the input
wave packets overlap; an even sign raises it. A correlated classical
router can emulate the former on the tested settings. A tested electronic
noise model, an exchange class of this graph, and a universal physical
spin–statistics law are three different claims.
