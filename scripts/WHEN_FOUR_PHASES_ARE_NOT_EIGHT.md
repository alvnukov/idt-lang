# A four-phase fringe does not fix the eight-phase response

A scheduled classical reader gets the bright-port probabilities 1, 1/2, 0, 1/2 at four equally spaced settings. The next four, placed *between* those settings, ask a different question: how much information from each arm can the reader use? The target for this comparison is the specified table p(x,y)=cos²(π(x−y)/8), x,y in {0,…,7}. A table used as a target is not itself an observation or a derivation.

## The four settings are actual contacts

The [four-phase circuit](probe_native_four_phase_gray.py) uses the same mobile four-label contact for preparation, arms, plates and recombination. It has three operating cells per arm and local fixed-zero references. The same token-carrying source participates in two fixed contact words which copy the prepared shades to the other arm; the token's path depends on the shades. Then a third independent fair shade selects its initial route. The local plate is reversible; on the blank sector its cycles have lengths 4 and 4, and on the one-token sector one 4-cycle moves the marker while the other eight states are fixed. Removing one operating cell and using just the specified two-cell generators gives 96 joint permutations and none with a four-cycle in **both** sectors. This is a restricted hardware comparison, not a general site-minimality claim.

For every pair of the four settings, a fixed native reader retrieves one bit from each arm and uses their equality to direct the token. The 1,536 launches (107,520 raw contacts) include three kinds of interception. With one full upstream or return shutter, the unconditional (left,right,dump) fractions are (1/4,1/4,1/2). Intercepting only the upstream token still dumps half, but its surviving half retains the fringe. Independent right-hand records flatten the fringe; shifting the prepared reference by one step shifts it. The final number reader, the contact program, the three fair inputs and clean cells are supplied.

## Drive a literal eighth-turn plate

The [eight-position circuit](probe_native_eighth_turn.py) gives a physical competitor to the cosine table. Four ordinary cyclic bits on an arm store a rotated half-bright clock. One local step shifts the four bits and complements the one crossing the end. The source produces a uniform clock phase from three independent fair shades, copies the prepared ordinary bits by contacts with the token, and uses a separate fair input for routing. The settings do not consult the hidden phase. With no shutters, the raw counts per 16 launches at successive relative settings are **16,12,8,4,0,4,8,12**. At one eighth-turn that is 3/4, not the target (2+√2)/4.

This result is replayed both on the four-label B contact and on the [three-label active contact](WHEN_THREE_MARKS_CAN_WRITE_A_CLICK.md). For ordinary blanks, three B bits give 48 signed permutations with no period-eight point. On the three-label law, four blanks give 192 even signed permutations with no such point. Adding a fifth ordinary site allows a five-contact eighth-turn plate; its extra bit flips to balance the parity. The full compilation uses restored ordinary catalyst cells and also balance cells that can retain a history. The token need not generate the oscillation: an optional change of encoding moves its marker but can be undone.

Including all settings and shutters gives 12,288 launches per implementation, 2,023,424 B or 16,293,888 ternary contacts, and maxima of 58 and 66 touched sites in a launch. With independent preparation on the two arms, 8,192 additional four-label launches are flat. A one-arm token-only shutter removes half the launches and preserves the surviving pattern; a full one-arm or return shutter gives (4,4,8)/16. This is a physical circuit **in the simulator**, with a supplied clock, stock, shutters and number meter; not a laboratory fringe or a derivation of the eight-angle target.

## What the intermediate settings require

In the [eight-setting capacity check](probe_eight_angle_reader_boundary.py) each arm sees only its own setting and a shared, setting-independent stock. It sends a symbol to a central yes/no decoder that does not otherwise see the settings. The comparison includes *every* launch. The even/even subtable is replayed with the four-phase circuit; the separate eight-position circuit above realizes the binary triangle on the full grid. The trit and 3×4 bounds below describe abstract reader classes, not compiled apparatus.

| Reader resources | Best possible maximum error on the 64 bright-port probabilities |
| --- | --- |
| One bit from either arm; even an unrestricted alphabet on the other | (√2−1)/4 ≈ 0.10355 |
| Three symbols from each arm; decoder may share the stock | (4√2−5)/16 ≈ 0.04105 |
| Three symbols from one arm, four from the other; fixed decoder and an externally weighted source | 0 |

For the equality-of-bits reader, consider (0,1), (0,7), (2,1), (2,7). The first three favor agreement, the last disagreement. Four local response bits can satisfy at most three of these conditions simultaneously. A uniformly rotated clock that is bright on four consecutive positions actually *attains* the one-bit error on the entire grid. For general central decoding, a separate exhaustive 4×4 mixed-setting check allows at most 12 correct binary-side labels or 13 correct trit-pair labels out of 16; the corresponding full-grid constructions attain the bounds. This uses a parity inequality inside one interferometer, not a Bell experiment with distant observers.

Why keep the richer reader? A common eight-position rotation and a fixed 3×4 Boolean decoder realize the target if supplied a mixing weight 2(√2−1). With 8n equiprobable stocks, rounding that weight gives maximum error at most 1/(16n). Neither the mixing weight nor a physical eight-phase apparatus is inferred from the fit. A missing setting, hidden timing message, direct access to the knob, or rejection of lost launches changes the inference. The counting helper reports descriptive all-launch fractions, not a confidence bound or a claim about laboratory data.

## Visibility, noise and a real measurement

For the noisy comparison p=(1+V cos(π(x−y)/4))/2, the exact maximum reproducible visibility is 1/√2 for a binary-side reader and 5√2/8 for two trits under the same stock-sharing contract. Both values are attained on all 64 cells. In the binary construction even the native triangle suffices **after** a supplied random relative shift: stay at zero with probability √2−1, otherwise shift by +1 or −1 equally. This removes the third harmonic. An externally chosen jitter distribution is not a physical derivation of a cosine. The [visibility controls](probe_phase_visibility_controls.py) also give the trit construction and a simpler shape test: for three consecutive calibrated settings starting at the aligned maximum, 2p₁−p₀−p₂ is zero for the static triangle and (√2−1)V/2 for the cosine. Misaligned or nonlinear controls invalidate that inference; an observed click fraction without calibrated simultaneous bounds is not a population exclusion.

[Brida et al., Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.5281#S3.F4) report one monitored interferometer output with a phase scan on one arm and another setting fixed or switched. This does not supply the independently varied, complete two-output grid above. An [experimental-model Comment](https://doi.org/10.7566/JPSJ.82.086001) and the [authors' Reply](https://doi.org/10.7566/JPSJ.82.086002) debate the interpretation of the internal device model; neither is a second experiment. We do not assign those data a new significance or use them to discard a contact law.
