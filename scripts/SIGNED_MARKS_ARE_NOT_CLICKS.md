# Signed marks are not clicks

Can a contact act as a wave mirror without storing a continuous amplitude or naming its other ports? For comparison, the Neumann vertex of a quantum graph already has scattering matrix `S = 2J/d − I`, where J is the all-ones matrix and d the degree: see [Kottos and Smilansky, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9812005). That result starts with waves on bonds and vertex boundary conditions. It does not establish an integer implementation or a particle detector.

Here is a discrete candidate. For an incident sign σ, return −σ to the same port and put 2σ in a common stock. On passing an integer multiple of d, broadcast one signed mark to each port. Cancel opposite marks at the same port within the same burst. The store only needs its remainder r in 0,…,d−1. Serialization, burst boundaries, signed marks and the ability to broadcast are supplied. The [local probe](probe_anonymous_signed_bucket.py) tests reversibility both of aggregate blocks and of framed atomic events. For aggregate inputs x, solve `2 sum(x) + r = d q + r′`; the output is `y = −x + q·1`. Applying the same aggregate rule to (y,r′) recovers (x,r). The signed sum including the stock is conserved. The decoded local quantity x + (r/d)·1 reflects exactly, and bare counters reflect exactly when the phase returns to its initial value zero (or, for arbitrary initial phase, over a closed integrated stream).

A [rotor rival](probe_signed_rotor_junction.py) returns the opposite sign to the input, sends two marks to consecutive ports and advances two positions; negative arrivals unwind the pointer. It needs a cyclic port ordering. The full-cycle signed wave agrees with the anonymous stock. For one positive ingress with d>2, however, the rotor always emits net signed flux +1; the stock emits −1 or d−1 regardless of initial phase. This discriminator requires a timed, sign-resolved whole burst. With two ports both mechanisms just exchange ports. For three positive arrivals at the same port with d=3, phase zero, both integrate to (−1,2,2). Counting surviving marks in the stock gives fractions (1/5,2/5,2/5); normalized squared field gives (1/9,4/9,4/9). Neither a single emitted click nor a detector that chooses with those squared probabilities follows.

**Networks.** On supports {0,1,2} and {1,2,3}, start with one unit in port 0 and both stocks empty. After the first contact the integer field is −e₀ and its stock is 2. The second contact changes no recorded variable. The ideal second reflection changes the field from (−1,2,2,0)/3 to (−3,2,2,8)/9. No function of this entire recorded state that agreed at the preceding step can give the correct wave at both times. In a different nested four-/five-port network every tenth pair of commands both stocks empty; the raw field drifts by (−1,−1,−1,−1,4) each such period while the ideal returns. The [network probe](probe_overlapping_bucket_dynamics.py) separates reachable errors from impossible starting residues over 814 directed contact pairs through five ports. Compatible supports still work; for any prespecified finite word, sufficiently amplifying the input can make each intermediate step exact. This extra gain does not rescue one fixed unit-gain primitive on every word. It also does not exclude other memory or local dynamics.

**Replay.** The `signed_contact_stream` checker compares literal per-port counters after individual steps or integrated prefixes. Fourteen [fixed specifications](../data/signed_contact_stream_controls.json) retain both outcomes, a two-port null, an inverse pair, a phase change, a memoryless wire and an intentionally mismatched single-event record. They are alternative synthetic observation contracts, not measurements from an instrument. An incomplete run cannot reject a candidate.
