# When one anonymous contact selects the square

**Candidate operation, not a physical axiom.** Give identical ports signed
rational scalar signals. If a memoryless contact is linear, equivariant
under all port permutations, reversible in one more application, fixes a
uniform input, and is nontrivial, then its matrix is forced. All diagonal
entries agree and all off-diagonal entries agree. On the difference space
the scalar action must be −1, on the uniform line +1. Thus each output is
twice the input mean minus its incoming signal. A norm was not an input
to that argument. Signed linear signals and the stated symmetries *were*.

### Determine an unknown readout without fitting an exponent

Let every port contribute the same unknown scalar function f to an
additive conserved response. Feed four ports `(t+2u,t,t,t)`. They return
`(t−u,t+u,t+u,t+u)`. Equality of the two totals is precisely a zero
third finite difference. On any integer grid, the only such tables are
quadratic plus linear plus constant. On all rational inputs, matching
the values at −1, 0 and 1 fixes the same polynomial on every rational
grid, without a continuity premise. Dark vacuum and sign insensitivity
leave f(x) proportional to x². This classifies a conserved additive
readout; it does not show that a measuring device emits a single event
with that frequency.

On the integer window −5 to 5, the executable comparison leaves 11
independent additive tables for a two-port swap, four for a three-port
integer contact (parity survives), and three for four ports. No nonconstant
real-valued additive table survives the four-port cyclic-field versions
tested. The presumed four-port law is **not a total integer operation**
on arbitrary inputs. If the local third difference has error at most ε,
the error relative to three nearby calibration points can grow as
binom(n+1,3) ε; one measured accuracy is not a uniform bound at every
amplitude.

### Prepare the tests using the same kind of port

For dyadic unit inputs, group the odd numerators by fours, equalize the
sum modulo four by an available sign flip, and apply a four-arm junction.
All numerators are then even. Iterating reduces the preparation to a
single occupied input; running the word in reverse prepares the test.
This is related to the [known dyadic orthogonal synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01175).
The probe pads four tested inputs with spectator squares and keeps a
blank sign-compensation port. On successive meshes it recovers unique
normalized responses on 7, 13, 25 and 49 table entries **when the same
local f applies across those preparations**. Even with dyadic gates one
must not assume arbitrary spectator equivalence: matched target signals
of 7/16 can have spectator numerator ideals generated by 3 versus 1.
Adding zero wires and applying reversible dyadic operations to the
spectators cannot identify these two examples.

There is a more general, but more expensive, route. With an isolated
signed integer magnitude m, a contact of degree 2m emits one branch of
magnitude m−1 and 2m−1 unit branches. Recursing yields exactly m² equal
unit-magnitude leaves. A rational normalized input with integer
numerators aᵢ and denominator q therefore refines into q² equal-magnitude
leaves, aᵢ² in group i. A single, exhaustive event with permutation- and
sign-symmetric response to the complete equal-leaf preparation would
give group i frequency aᵢ²/q² if grouping preserves the marginal.
Equal amplitudes alone do not imply event symmetry. A retained branch
tag or a lossy readout can break it. The [equal-branch reasoning of
Zurek](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0405161) uses entangled Schmidt
records and environmental swaps, not this network of real junctions.

The sharp necessary maximum port degree for this isolated m-split, when
every smaller degree may be used, is max(4, largest prime divisor of m)
for m>1. With arbitrary valence, sign control and blank auxiliary modes,
split each component of a nonzero integer direction w into its square
number of leaves, apply one global equal junction, then undo the split.
The result on **any** input is the rational reflection about w, with all
initially blank auxiliaries restored. At most n reflections suffice for
an n-dimensional rational orthogonal map. A fixed finite rational gate
alphabet cannot create arbitrary new denominator primes. Unsigned stars
and wire permutations by themselves preserve the signed sum; they cannot
implement every reflection on single wires. A differential two-wire
encoding trades a sign control for the preparation of a balanced pair.

### A nonlinear rival that passes the same readout tests

Act linearly near inputs with only two distinct signal levels; otherwise
leave the input unchanged. The switch uses squared distance from the
two-level inputs, divided by total variance. It is invariant under
simultaneous shifts, rescaling, permutations and the reflection itself.
The resulting nonlinear involution conserves signed sum and squared power
and passes **every** localized-input, square-refinement and third-difference
test above on a single splitting branch. Conservation of the square does
not establish linear dynamics.

A three-port localized source gives `(−1,2,2,0)/3` before the next
four-port command. With the independently specified switch threshold
1/27 this preparation lies at distance 2/27: the predictions for **power
in ports 0 and 3 relative to incident power** are 17/18 and 1/9. No
survivor normalization or assumption about individual clicks is needed
for that wave test. This is a proposed finite discriminator, not an
observation. The threshold defines an adversarial candidate, not a value
fitted to nature.
