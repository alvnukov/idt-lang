# One event needs more than a conserved square

Suppose port i with signed integer field xᵢ offers ordered pairs of internal addresses, each in 0,…,|xᵢ|−1. There are xᵢ² such **tickets**. That is a candidate microscopic interpretation, not a result of these tests. The [unsigned edge-packet comparison](probe_unsigned_pair_tickets.py) actually forms pairs in a more restricted preparation model; neither construction derives uniform sampling. What would it take to carry *one* ticket, unchanged as an event identity but possibly moved to another port, through the four-port reflection?

**Orientation.** An input (2,0,0,0) has four tickets at its only active port. Its reflected field (−1,1,1,1) has one ticket at each port. Any relabeling of the three empty input ports fixes every original ticket, but moves some required output tickets. Therefore no reversible port-anonymous bijection using just the original tickets can do the job. Appending six abstract names which permutations leave untouched changes nothing.

In the specified four-port model, a reusable orientation needs at least six possible states. For a reflection-invariant domain of 401 integer fields the [event-transport probe](probe_equivariant_event_tickets.py) constructs actual equivariant involutions, not just equal counts: 21,024 joint states per six-state scheme. One can keep an undirected ring fixed if the arrow around it may reverse; an oriented ring whose arrow must stay fixed fails on the alternating field. A different six-state reference, an ordering of the three pair partitions, can remain unchanged. The algebra does not initialize a reference from symmetric empty ports. Choosing a ring uniformly also need not make a fixed input ticket land uniformly.

**A counting clock.** Demand additionally that a *fixed* reference run one directed cycle through every ticket at each fixed, nonempty field. Now each admissible reference orbit has at least 12 states. At the all-ones field its remaining symmetries must commute with a four-cycle; leaving a whole cyclic group of order four still fails reversible transport on the alternating field. The [12-state construction](probe_autonomous_ticket_clock.py) checks 4,800 nonempty clocks on the bounded domain, and makes the tick and scattering commute. It supplies a nonlocal ordering of tickets and a chosen initial state. It gives the square counts over an exact whole cycle at fixed preparation, not independent samples. Advancing both commands every time can sample only two of four output ports in the scattered preparation. These minima apply to the stated anonymous, reusable architectures, not to all possible detectors.

**A measured discriminator.** In the public [Münzberg et al. histogram](../data/pump_hbt/README.md), two detectors observed a passive splitter fed by a resonantly pulsed quantum dot. If one pump pulse always advances a fixed deterministic four-tick clock by one, and each clock position maps to the same one of the two arms, opposite arms cannot both get a clean detection separated by four pump periods. Deleting detections cannot turn a same-arm pair into an opposite-arm pair. Integrating actual bins within ±1.5 ns of the two predicted delays gives 64,584 and 64,452 counts; together these are 99.2% of the mean-normalized adjacent third/fifth peaks. The [offline replay](probe_pump_locked_hbt.py) verifies source bytes and neighboring windows. This is a comparison with the **clean pump-locked static-arm model**, not a rejection of all ticket clocks. Backgrounds or incorrect assignments can create counted pairs; missed source ticks or phases which change with emission alter the prediction. No numerical upper bound on all such effects is supplied here. A seven-state deterministic control can give equal side-peak rates at all six comparison lags; those bins do not certify independence.

An informative follow-up would count four **independently heralded inputs**, not merely four pump periods; retain all herald IDs including no-clicks and double-clicks, both detector time tags, clock gaps and resets. Predefine disjoint timing windows and a bound on spurious pairs. Without that evidence distinguishability stays conditional, and absence of a discrepancy is not a confirmation of the primitive.

**When could the fine exit already be recorded?** Keep the preparation fixed and independently intervene on a late gate. If qᵢ of all trials already carry a permanent, readable, non-disturbing exit label i, then qᵢ cannot exceed the minimum final probability of i over those interventions. Summing the minima gives a sharp capacity for these tables, not an observation of when a click happens. In the [three-bank circuit](probe_irrevocable_event_prefix.py) every *pair* of late choices leaves positive mass in common, but all three together share no fine exit. The coarse bank can nevertheless be recorded on every trial. If each of the three rows may move by total variation ε≤1/9, the maximal common fine mass is exactly 3ε. In a different two-choice tree the capacities rise from 1/9 to 5/9 to 1 as settings arrive. An inaccessible provisional label, a detector that changes the future interference, and a setting correlated with early state do **not** obey the passive-record interpretation. The probe keeps explicit controls for those alternatives.
