# What does a reversal or a setting comparison actually test?

Two checks start from the same [finite packet instruments](WHEN_TWO_PACKETS_CAN_MAKE_ONE_RECORD.md). They ask different questions: does a reversal of the program represent an experimentally calibrated reversal, and did the settings reach the source before the readout? Neither check assumes the packet is a neutron or a photon.

### Neutron beta decay: a missing prediction is not a prediction of zero

Use two detector arms and two recorded spin settings. For positive background-subtracted, exposure-corrected rates form the cross ratio R=(r++ r--)/(r+- r-+) and the signed contrast (1−√R)/(1+√R), using the adapter's arm orientation. Separate arm efficiencies and spin-dependent common factors cancel. Reversing just one label reverses the contrast; reversing both does not. Synthetic controls recover 0, +1/5 and −1/5 exactly. A **joint** spin–arm efficiency, however, produces the *identical four cells* with zero underlying contrast as a physical-looking +1/5 signal. An independent isotropic calibration with the same response distinguishes the two examples; without it they remain ambiguous.

Measured neutron asymmetries provide a target independent of this construction. Cold-beam [PERKEO III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04666) reported A₀ = −0.11985 ± 0.00017 (stat.) ± 0.00012 (syst.); stored-ultracold-neutron [UCNA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00884) reported −0.12054 ± 0.00044 ± 0.00068. These are published corrected coefficients, **not** four raw rates, and the probe fits neither experiment. In the packet model, inverting a menu exchanges two signed coefficients (+2 and −2), but the corresponding positive counts are both 2. There is no supplied calibration linking this operation to axial neutron spin, polar electron momentum, or four predicted rates. The comparison therefore returns *not evaluable*, not zero and not an exclusion of the underlying primitive.

### Network settings: a positive table is not a free-setting model

For the three-CHSH network functional T, unrestricted choices of local deterministic readers on the finite packet addresses obey T ≤ 6 when the addresses are independent of the settings. The native four-way readout on the 256 address pairs realizes 32 distinct strategies (eight copies each). Every address wins nine of the twelve active setting pairs; at each setting exactly 192 addresses win and 64 lose.

As a countercontrol, **tell the source both settings**. Sample each winning address with probability p/192 and each losing address with (1−p)/64, for 3/4 ≤ p ≤ 1. Then T=12(2p−1). The checker forwards every address through each of the two supplied instruments. The resulting observable tables are normalized and have nonsignalling marginals. This does not restore setting independence. At p=7/8 the result is T=9, requiring 0.06959337 bit of setting information on average per uniformly chosen active pair (0.04639558 bit per setting when all 18 are uniform and the six inactive ones are independent). The active-setting cost is the binary relative entropy D(p‖3/4) in bits: coarse-grain to win/loss for the lower bound; the two constant likelihood ratios attain it.

The two sources can be independent **conditional on a given setting pair** in this example. A stronger demand is that each source depends *only on its own side's setting*. Every per-address 2×2 probability table on a rectangular set of settings must then have rank at most one. At p=7/8 all 768 checked rectangles in the saturating construction have absolute determinant 7/589824, not zero. The equality condition likewise excludes saturation by such a side-local model at p>3/4 within this deterministic address comparison. This does not give the optimum cost under every causal structure.

[Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04177) reported T=7.83(3) and [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08123) reported 8.09(1). **No source dependence in either experiment was measured by this probe.** The cost above is for a deliberately constructed alternative, not a diagnosis of experimental leakage. For related setting-dependence costs for a single CHSH comparison and the importance of the assumed causal direction, see [Hall and Branciard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11903).
