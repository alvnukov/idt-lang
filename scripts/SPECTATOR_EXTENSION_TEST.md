# An extra mode turns four-mode ambiguity into a test

The [finite alternative recorder](WHAT_NATIVE_FRAMES_CAN_MISS.md)
was not rejected in its four-mode domain. Here the question is whether
*that same calibrated response* survives when empty modes, different
implementations of the same event, and one exclusive output instrument
are required to agree. Tests below are synthetic and exact, not new
laboratory measurements.

### Six ports: no pair-basis measurement needed

Start in mode 0. An equal three-arm junction on 0,1,2 and a four-arm
junction on 1,3,4,5 produce the signed pattern **(−1,−1,2,1,1,1)/3**.
For each of its five odd positions, apply only operations on *other*
ports. They compress the output to three occupied ports, with one odd
entry at the tagged position. The four-mode residue response would give
certainty at that tag. They are five distinct tags of the **same**
original six-output setting: their total cannot be five.

The comparison does not require a continuum of rotations. For each
tag, a full matrix identity rewrites the physical word as a small
three-port preparation preceded by operations **entirely on initially
empty ports**. The latter leave the initial wave unchanged. A one-bit
countermodel preserves all five operator identities but records a
consequential trace of operations on empty ports. Such devices cannot
be excluded simply by calling the residual preparations equal.
An incoherent independent-router composition is also different from
a reversible wave circuit. The ordinary squared-amplitude model passes
the same comparison with five probabilities 1/9 and the remaining 4/9.

The five transferred probabilities would need total tolerance at least
four, or **4/5 per transfer as a common bound**; equality is attainable.
If each setting keeps its own favorable fifth of all attempts, each
conditional can be one without a single inconsistent raw count. Never
substitute those conditional frequencies for the five unconditional ones.

### Five ports: a common two-dimensional outcome matters

One extra port already suffices if an output plane can be resolved
both by its two coordinate rays and by their symmetric and antisymmetric
combinations. Nineteen explicit native word checks transfer calibrated
events. With the specified four-mode responses, the five output
probabilities of a single preparation are forced to
**9/16, 1/16, 1/16, 9/16, 1/4**. Their sum is 3/2. The same word and
coarse-outcome identities admit normalized ordinary squared overlaps.
If key shared-plane identifications are dropped, the finite constraints
become feasible again. A separate five-dimensional coordinate-only
countercontrol explains why the mixed readout cannot be silently omitted.

### A cheap reusable necessary test

The `operational_probability_ledger` checker accepts labelled complete
rows of exclusive outcomes, exact bounds on sums, and **declared**
equalities or tolerances between events. It knows nothing about
Householder matrices, residue arithmetic or particle names. Its decision
is accompanied by either an explicit nonnegative normalized distribution
or a rational weighted contradiction that can be checked against the
original input. If the work budget runs out, it reports inconclusive.
Different postselected populations cannot be connected by an unconditional
transfer. The ten archived designs separate both five- and six-mode
failures, passing comparators, sharp tolerance boundaries and missing
experimental assumptions. Feasibility establishes only consistency of
those finite submitted requirements, not physical truth.
