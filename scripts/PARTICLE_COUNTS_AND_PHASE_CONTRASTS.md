# Particle counts are three different experimental questions

The number of locally named states, the number of active propagation
frequencies, and the total strength of invisible neutral currents should
not be made into the same checker input. A fourth question, violation of
charge–parity symmetry (CP), tests interactions or their dictionary,
not the number of sheets in a graph. The probes below compare specific
models and observations without fitting IDT parameters.

**Invisible Z width.** With a purely left-handed Dirac current at tree
level, define x,y as squared masses in units of the squared pole mass.
For an open two-body channel, the relative kinematic factor is
√[(1−x−y)²−4xy] [1−(x+y)/2−(x−y)²/2]; at or beyond threshold it is zero.
Rest-frame and invariant evaluations agree. With three massless states
and one at 0.6 Z masses, the same rank-three coupling projector gives
3 massless units when the heavy state is sterile, 2 when fully active,
and 2.6323175424 for one rational mixing. The heavy pair cannot be
produced, but the two ordered heavy–light channels can. A decay counted
as *invisible* also needs a detector/decay assumption.

[CMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07110) obtained 523 ± 3 (stat) ± 16
(syst) MeV; [ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02789), 506 ± 2 ± 12
MeV. Their charged-dilepton references and conversion to MeV are not
identical. The [updated LEP lineshape analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02067)
gives 2.9963 ± 0.0074 conventional light-neutrino units; it reuses LEP
data and a Standard Model width ratio. None is a literal state counter.
No approximate mass limit, threshold exclusion or joint fit is made here.

**Flavour oscillations.** A shared two-state vacuum mass generator has
one nonzero squared-mass difference regardless of the preparation or
mixing angle. The [KamLAND-only result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.4667)
is (7.54 +0.19/−0.18) × 10⁻⁵ eV²; [Daya Bay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14988)
finds an effective short-baseline splitting (2.519 ± 0.060) × 10⁻³ eV².
At 1,650 m and 4 MeV the slow-scale deficit stays below 0.17% over its
printed error interval even at maximal mixing; the fast central term
is 7.98%. This is a common-ruler model comparison, not a recorded bin;
Daya Bay includes the solar term and an external solar splitting.
[JUNO's later analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37895) also uses a
Daya Bay constraint when resolving fast-frequency minima.

An exact synthetic trace violates the one-frequency recurrence. But the
**same trace** is reproduced at all synthetic ticks by a single generator
and unresolved path lengths L and 2L. Baselines, energy calibration,
matter, source weights and selection must be matched before counting
frequencies. Under the common-generator conditions, two distinct resolved
gaps require at least three participating mass states. A fourth, fully
decoupled state can leave both the widths and oscillations unchanged.
Neither comparison counts complete charged families.

**Direct CP violation.** [NA48](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/0208009)
reports Re(ε′/ε) = (14.7 ± 2.2) × 10⁻⁴ for its full data set and
[KTeV](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0127) (19.2 ± 2.1) × 10⁻⁴ for its
independent measurement. The double ratio of pion-channel widths for
long- and short-lived kaons tests a common indirect-mixing explanation.
Different beams, regeneration, backgrounds and acceptance corrections
matter; the quoted fits are not four raw flavour-tagged counts.

A separate two-path construction exposes the distinction: unequal
conjugate **partial** rates need both a weak- and a strong-phase
difference in this model, whereas a double ratio can depart from one
even if the flavour-tagged partial rates are equal. A real representation
with doubled coordinates and a specified complex structure reproduces
the same rates. The data do not establish that the fundamental records
are complex, or fix the number of families without a specified field
and coupling model.
