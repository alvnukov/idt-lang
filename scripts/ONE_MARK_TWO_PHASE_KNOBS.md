# One mark, two phase knobs

Can one classical mark, rather than a stored amplitude or a branching cloud, explain the response of a three-port junction? There is a positive result for a single device. Give the incoming port i two hidden port labels a,b. If (i,a,b) has two distinct names, exchange the two names throughout the triple; if all three are different, exchange the first two slots; otherwise do nothing. The first output slot is the outgoing port. This rule is reversible and independent of port names. Uniformly preparing the nine ordered pairs (a,b) gives the profile of a squared trivalent wave reflection: 1/9 back to the input, 4/9 to either other port. Firing twice with the updated state returns the entire triple exactly. With a fresh pair instead, the return probability is only 11/27. Uniform seeds and their physical availability are not explained.

The [one-mark probe](probe_single_mark_ternary_echo.py) enumerates all 940 equivariant involutions on three port slots. Some 160 match the same one-contact profile. For the stated rule, two overlapping contacts give 32/81 on the last port with independent seeds. Even allowing arbitrary correlations while preserving uniform seed marginals gives a sharp range **2/9 to 5/9**; the wave comparator gives 64/81. An alternative rule allowed to invent a previously absent port label can counterfeit the bright rate with correlated seeds. A further known-seed calibration can separate this broader class, but is not accessible merely from the public one-port frequencies. None of the single-contact matches selects the seed rule in nature.

**A comparison without preparing or reading the hidden seeds.** Put two independent late phase controls on distinct internal paths. Suppose exactly one carrier can visit at most one control, the path is chosen before the settings, an unused path carries no side channel, and arbitrary hidden source/detector randomness is independent of the settings. Then the four unconditioned full output rows obey `p00 + p11 = p10 + p01`, component by component. Conversely, every set of four normalized nonnegative distributions obeying that identity has such a one-visit *table representation*. This is a characterization of this operational contract; it need not describe a unique mechanism.

For the two actual ternary contacts, the two equal-setting rows are P=(9,4,4,64)/81; the two different-setting rows are Q=(9,36,36,0)/81. No intermediate path probability is required. Their total variation is 64/81. The best one-visit approximation to all four rows needs a common TV tolerance at least 32/81, and that value is attained. If the data were exact, at least 64/81 of a setting-independent mixture would have to be assigned to a mechanism not obeying the one-visit restriction. A phase record transported by an apparently empty arm is one possible escape; direct setting leakage or biased conditioning would also change the contract. The [phase-budget probe](probe_one_visit_phase_budget.py) separates these controls. The four probabilities are not observations, and the single-device setting geometry is not a Bell experiment.

**Can independent local absorption of the same field produce exactly one click?** Illuminate one of the three possible station pairs AB, AC, BC. Route the same two half-amplitude rails to each lit station and check the local field inputs, not only their sum. If a lit station responds only to its own setting-independent input and possibly globally shared source randomness, then on a given trial there are three putative lit-response bits. At most two of the three pairs have exactly one click. The triangle bound is known (see [Liang, Spekkens and Wiseman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1273)); here the [absorber probe](probe_three_absorber_menus.py) adds the explicit rail preparation and a sharp loss comparison. Up to mean efficiency 2/3 the local model can reproduce every row of a symmetric one-particle router with losses. Above it, at fixed mean number η≤1 of lit clicks, at least η/2−1/3 of trials have two lit clicks. Do not discard zero- or two-click trials.

Identical incident fields need not mean identical microscopic inputs: a token traveling on one of the original rails is routed by the switches and can arrive at precisely one station without any remote detector signal. This example preserves an actual candidate that a field-only detector test must not eliminate. None of these tables resolves how the uniform initial preparation, coherent phase transport and a single absorption arise together.
