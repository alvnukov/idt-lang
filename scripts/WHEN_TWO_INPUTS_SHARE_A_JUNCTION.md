# When two inputs share a junction

Do not infer exchange statistics by looking only at where two localized excitations end up. A more economical first test is to calibrate each input separately and check whether the two-input response is even compatible with the *same noninteracting device*.

### Two packets on the free-pivot crossing

An isolated two-bond packet entering the [four-port junction](WHEN_A_SECOND_JUNCTION_DISTINGUISHES_MODELS.md) at the even offset has zero probability of a **first gate hit** on its injection arm; each of the other three gates is hit first with probability 1/3 under uniform choice among allowed moves. This is not a ban on later visits: on the (31,37)-site wedge, with no absorbing meter, the earliest reachable return to input 0's own outward gate takes 126 events. Prepare two such packets on distinct input ports, using exactly the same bonds and pivot rule. The rule has no packet names. A passive 29-event sequence puts two separate outward-facing two-bond figures on **one of the injection arms**, at offsets 4 and 10. There is no detector interaction in that sequence. Its tenth move is not offered by either of the two tracked pairs in isolation. The sequence and its complete offer menus replay for all 12 ordered input choices on two wedge sizes. Under the specified sampler, **one** such prefix has probability 1/24,461,180,928; this is not the rate of the full event.

A separate, consuming meter removes a clean outward pair at its first encounter with an established gate. On the (31,37)-site wedge, for each of the six distinct input pairs and each of its originally occupied ports, the unconditioned probability of recording two arrivals there by event 24 is 11,262,469,367 / 10,749,542,400,000 (about 0.00105). By event 36 roughly 0.29844 of injections have two readings, 0.35206 are jammed and 0.34949 are still unresolved. No surviving-click normalization or inferred long-time limit enters the result.

With independently propagated packets this particular double return has probability zero: one packet would have to take its own dark exit. It is also zero in a passive one-body linear mode transformation with zero amplitude for that transition, regardless of exchange sign. Both comparisons require the **same one-body calibration for each input and additive counting at the same output ports**. An interacting device, a different joint detector, or unmatched internal input modes need not obey them. The moving bond shapes have not been identified as photons, electrons, or fermions. [Executable raw witness and unconditioned two-hit census](probe_two_packet_dark_return.py).

### Apparent exclusion with two uncolored tokens

Use a different law, the [original four-label reciprocal splitter](WHEN_A_COLORLESS_TOKEN_READS_TWO_ARMS.md). Inject at its left D only, right D only, or both D cells; use the same independent shade stock on E in every case. Read all four sites, including E. Both single-particle distributions are uniform. With both inputs occupied, exactly one token comes out in each arm, the four cross-arm pairs equally probable. This baseline even admits two orthogonal one-particle amplitude columns (1,1,1,1)/2 and (1,1,−1,−1)/2 whose antisymmetrized pair gives the same four counts. Those columns are **a supplied comparator**, not amplitudes obtained from the raw rule.

Append just two contacts: touch the left E with its zero reference, then contact left and right E. **Both one-particle site tables remain uniform**, yet the mean two-particle occupations at (left D, left E, right D, right E) become (2,3,2,1)/4. The measured mean number on the left is 5/4, not 1. Mirroring the contacts reverses the excess; reversing their time order does not preserve the individual calibrations.

For a fixed passive one-body mode transformation, two independently occupied, distinct input modes have an additive one-body density: their **mean** counts add even when exchange interference changes their *coincidences*. This assumes a matched one-body preparation, unchanged device/background and the same additive number readout. The above result is incompatible with that contract; it does not exclude interacting quantum particles or identify a statistic from a saturated detector. A hard cap of one mark per *raw site* alone is not Pauli exclusion for a calibrated physical mode.

The split/append calculation uses exactly four equiprobable preparations, no postselection. Reversibility keeps the two-input preparations distinct. Only two states of the reachable 13-state sector place both tokens on the left, so its mean count cannot exceed 3/2. A six-contact suffix attains this while preserving both uniform single-input calibrations. Exhaustive finite word search verifies that length two is the first calibrated nonadditivity and length six the first attainment of 3/2 on this fixed five-edge apparatus. [Raw comparison and independent permutation-matrix checker](probe_colorless_pair_additivity.py).

### A packet count is not a current-noise measurement

The minimally modified four-label splitter gives left count X=1 on three shade preparations and X=2 on the fourth. Its one-launch variance is 3/16. Keep **all the same single-launch histograms** and change only how the sources provide the four shades across launches. For M launches, the variance of their summed left count is 3M/16 with independent redraws, 3M²/16 if one random shade pair is frozen for the entire run, and r(4−r)/16 if the four pairs cycle in order from a uniform random starting position, where r is M modulo 4. In the frozen case this is an ensemble variance, not variation seen over time in one run. A saturated detector recording only whether X is positive clicks on every launch in all three cases. The [time-window and exact covariance control](probe_packet_current_noise.py) checks what is lost by either changing the detector or ignoring correlations between launches.

For comparison, [Bocquillon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.7093) varied the relative timing of two electronic sources and measured low-frequency **excess current noise** at an output. They report a fitted overlap parameter γ=0.45±0.05 and a fitted emission time of 62±10 ps. Neither quantity is a count of double returns in this simulator. To compare, one would need an independently specified mapping from native launches and detector records to physical current, pulse duration, repetition, noise normalization and the electronic background. No fitted γ is inserted into the binary dynamics, and no agreement or disagreement with that experiment is claimed.
