# What a signal can measure without starting with coordinates

The previous [relational observer probe](RELATIONAL_OBSERVERS_AND_COVERS.md) found three independent abelian return records after weakening selected square reunions. Three independent counters are not a three-dimensional Euclidean space. We now ask what two actual signal protocols on the *same* retained relations would report.

**Arrival front versus spread.** In the rooted cover, give each site nine possible dispatches: one per reciprocal hop, the rest holds. The rival doubles the tickets on the three marked root edges, compensating with fewer holds. Every previously possible hop or hold is still possible in one tick. Thus the two worlds have identical timed paths, not just the same leading wavefront. A local recorder at the marked root counts elapsed ticks and signed mark crossings on returns. Enumerating all 6,561 four-tick schedules gives square-score totals (contrast across marked counters, sum of marked counters) of (20,12) versus (64,24); missing returns are retained. These ratios are neither lengths measured with an external ruler nor count rates from a laboratory. Uniform dispatch tickets are an extra law.

The [signal-metric probe](probe_relational_signal_metric.py) computes the long-time covariance two ways, by a cell correction to the count process and by first-return statistics. The return contrast/sum ratio changes from 5/3 to 30/13. Changes along the one sum and two transverse modes have different factors, so changing a common tick duration cannot remove the difference. If instead dispatch excludes the incoming port, even the two-tick return changes from 1/5 to zero, although shortest possible arrivals still agree. Only for that second comparison is the long-time change a single overall factor. The observer needs a recorder for the accumulated marks; a diffuse cover with no preferred common root needs more instrumentation. We obtain neither a unique common metric for all signals nor physical motion, Euclidean isotropy, Lorentz symmetry or gravity.

**Will clocks agree on what counts as a phase?** A cyclic shift on three slots has a centered rational plane on which T²+T+I=0; four slots also have a plane on which T²=−I, plus a distinct parity mode. These identities expose different rotations without calling any raw mark a complex amplitude. Suppose a fixed, real-linear coupling must commute with one common tick of two clocks of lengths m,n. Its cross-weights can depend only on the phase difference modulo gcd(m,n); transfer rank is at most that gcd. A wired equal-port contact attains the bound. Three and four slots cannot exchange a nonconstant mode through such a *static* interface even if both are reset together. An external converter cycling through 6 settings can pass the four-clock parity mode from a centered three-clock input; transferring its full two-dimensional signal into the four-clock rotation plane needs a converter period divisible by 12 in this model.

The [clock probe](probe_relational_clock_modes.py) uses the same twelve-slot source with alternative receivers: the three-slot receiver accepts the period-three mode and rejects the period-four one; the four-slot receiver swaps those roles. Rejection is reflection in the lossless full circuit, not disappearance. A reversed wiring changes orientation, and averaging over independent origins can wash out a signal; neither is the same as incompatible periods. The linearity, interface wiring, shared tick when used, and measurement of signed signal are supplied. Neither result resolves where an internal observer or its clock comes from.
