# A positive response in every native frame is not enough

This is a test of the **measuring apparatus** as much as of a candidate.
The marked signed carrier admits a real four-mode representation. Its six
compatible contexts give 24 rank-one readouts and nine binary questions.
The 24 responses span all ten real symmetric matrix coordinates. Thus a
failure below is neither a missing matrix coefficient nor an inaccurate fit.

## One added measurement breaks a false positive

There is a trace-one real quadratic response whose minimum on those 24
rays is **+4/315**, and whose responses sum to one in each context. No
longer finite sequence of the same signed gates and native Lüders tests
exposes it: pulling any native rank-one effect backwards through a gate or
projector yields another such effect times a nonnegative scalar, or zero.
This closure statement applies under the specified instruments, including
adaptive choices; it is not a theorem about arbitrary detectors.

Add a junction on three of the four channels. Equal wave impedances,
continuity at the node and balance of incoming and outgoing flux give
reflected amplitude −1/3 and two transmitted amplitudes 2/3: in matrix
form the active block is 2J/3−I, where J is the all-ones matrix. For an
example of these **supplied boundary conditions**, see [Kottos and
Smilansky](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9812005). In one step the new
readout gives the adversarial quadratic response **−1/35**, which cannot
be an event probability. With signed port operations these junctions
also generate dense real rotations in the usual closure topology: the
checked two-plane rotations have twice-cosine −14/9, and their conjugate
axes span the rotations on each triple. Without the signs the same
junctions fix the uniform vector. Importing an arbitrary beam splitter
would bypass precisely the apparatus question being tested.

## Dense directions do not impose a continuous rule of probability

An alternative finite recorder agrees with **every** one of the 24 initial
ray preparations and measurements, yet makes a different one-step
prediction. Write an accessible ray as a primitive integer vector.
Keep its odd entries only, reducing their signs modulo four. These
signed patterns are just 24 rays. Using their squared overlaps instead
of the squared overlaps of the input directions gives a symmetric,
normalized transition table. Each accessible ray can prepare its own
certain outcome; resetting to the selected ray supplies a repeatable
sequence of complete sharp tests. All complete orthogonal bases of
admissible rays normalize, even if their elements came from different
circuit words, and equal coarse effects have equal probabilities. A
coarse report after a fine measurement is *not* a nondestructive
measurement of the higher-rank subspace.

The congruence behind this finite recorder is special. Native directions
in four modes have squared length a square or twice a square, after
clearing rational denominators. If their primitive vector has k odd
entries (k=1, 2, or 4), its norm squared is k times an **odd** square.
Four times the alternative overlap is the residue of four times the
ordinary projector overlap modulo eight. The program checks 28,032
necessary congruence representatives, not 28,032 independent laboratory
predictions. [Meyer's three-dimensional rational coloring](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9905080)
is a mathematical precedent, not this four-mode model or a physical
explanation.

After one equal three-port junction, ordinary squared amplitudes for a
calibrated single input give **1/9 at its original port, 4/9 at each
other active port**; the finite recorder gives **certainty at the
original port**. It treats the three-arm junction as idle on its 24
projective records. Both hypotheses must remain distinguishable in the
history. In particular, dense rational directions, matching the initial
tomography, reciprocity and repeatability do not enforce continuity.
With a *continuous quadratic* response, by contrast, nonnegativity on
the dense orbit implies positivity on its closure.

Applying the same scattering law on all four ports introduces a new
operator but permutes the original six frames and normalizes the old
gate set. It does not remove the alternative law; neither does merely
allowing every rational orthogonal four-mode gate. A quite different
resource is **measuring** the uniform active eigenmode of a three-port
junction with the bypass resolved; its primitive norm squared is three.
Performing the junction is not automatically such a measurement.

## How not to mistake a calibration for an event result

In an ideal 900-attempt design, the ordinary *expected* port counts are
100/400/400, not a mandatory exact outcome. A shunted node can instead
give 225/225/225 with 225 missed attempts; survivor normalization would
make it look perfectly balanced. Two coherent junction passes undo the
first transformation, unlike two independent random routings with its
single-pass intensities. Classical wave power can reproduce that
two-pass interference. Port-resolved detection, loss, multiple clicks,
phase calibration, input preparations and trial denominators still matter.

The measured [three-port interferometer of Osawa et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02033)
was balanced near one third per output, not this 1/9–4/9 device.
[Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13473) characterized a different
three-port matrix. The [four-port Grover–Michelson experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04303)
used continuous-wave power and two terminated legs; terminating just
one leg is a proposed distinct way to obtain the three-port transformation.
These papers do not supply raw single-event evidence selecting the
1/9–4/9 law for this candidate IDT device.
