# When packet order generates a quarter turn

There is a way for the order of requests to affect *unsigned occupancy itself*. It does not start with a signed amplitude. Give each site two places. One site contains `11`, the rest `00`. A context pairs up all sites without leaving one out. Of all the four-place contacts in that matching, just one is half filled. Complementing its four occupations moves the packet along the matching. No null firing is counted. The choice of context, the incidence, and the one-packet preparation are supplied; the choice of **which contact** fires is not separately scheduled.

**Two contexts.** On an alternating ring of eight sites, write A and B for its two matchings; words below are read from left to right. ABBA returns the packet. ABAB sends every starting site to its antipode, Z; this change commutes with both controls and doing it twice returns the packet. Eight is the smallest number of sites for a nontrivial central elementary commutator in this two-perfect-matching class. On a connected ring of 2m sites, AB has order m: it advances two places in opposite directions on the two parities. Its square is central and nontrivial exactly at m=4. On a larger system, powers or a selected subspace can behave differently.

An isolated anonymous packet on a bare ring does not label its starting place. Attaching a passive `01` pair at one vertex provides a relational readout: its contact with a `00` or `11` site never fires. Starting next to the spur, the final distance is 1 after ABBA, 5 after ABAB, but 3 after either AB or BA. These are distances on the supplied graph; a physical distance meter is not derived. [Literal four-place updates, ring census and controls](probe_packet_matching_order.py).

**Three contexts.** Require a simple connected graph with three colored perfect matchings. Every two colors must give alternating octagons and no uncolored simple circuit may have length below six. These are structural assumptions that can be tested without reading a complex amplitude. They do not explain why nature would meet them.

At eight sites there is no solution: a third edge inside an octagon either repeats an edge or closes a circuit of length at most five. At 16 sites the first two colors give two octagons. Every remaining edge must join the two. Of all 40,320 cross connections, 552 have all the required two-color periods, 16 give the required uncolored girth, and eight have both. Those eight are the same colored construction up to renaming sites. A different matching on an isomorphic *uncolored* graph does not have the same two-color periods: context labels matter.

On the smallest solution the words ABAB, ACAC and BCBC act as the **same** fixed-point-free switch Z. The word W=ABC commutes with all three controls and W²=Z. Compare counts at a site and its Z-partner: on these signed differences Z acts as negation, hence W² acts as minus the identity. This is a real-linear complex structure on a specified readout. On the underlying addresses W is still an ordinary order-four permutation. With two contexts AB already squared to Z; the new fact is a **word commuting with all the local controls**. Swapping two context names reverses the sign convention on the odd sector.

Minimal size is not a harmless convention. A constructed *connected* 32-site cover has the same shortest uncolored circuit and all the same two-color octagonal periods, but the three square words from one site end at two distinct addresses. Also, on eight sites some completions satisfy W²=Z without supplying the three required pair squares. Neither a single successful word nor local circuit lengths on an unknown-size system select the 16-site law. [Complete finite search and larger countermodel](probe_octagon_three_contexts.py).

This supplies a candidate origin for a small operator algebra on differences of occupations, not a derivation of the quantum state, its global-phase equivalence, interference, or the probability of a detection. A direct address detector could see a change which a quantum global phase would hide. Two copies do not acquire a shared phase convention solely because they obey the same local rule.
