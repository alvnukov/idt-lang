# What can a local observer actually distinguish?

The `relational_germ_observer` checker now accepts a raw reciprocal
adjacency table, an actual two-step marked path, two Boolean recorder rules,
and separately specified integer reference intervals. It transports a port
by inspecting the fourth corner of a local square, explores the reachable
port/bit states and counts pair ballots. It has no access to hidden cube
coordinates or a particle dictionary. Eight compiled experimental designs
are pinned with their original predictions, separately from the descriptive
research archive.

On the 16-vertex folded graph the nontrivial flat recorder takes 80 product
states and 400 local transfers to verify the 16 reported integers. The wrong
recorder gives two explicit paths to the same vertex with different final
bits. A constant bit matches a zero-only comparator, but that uninformative
success does not match the six-level target. A missing or ambiguous fourth
corner is *unsupported*, not evidence against a physical theory. A further
control has 96 origin/vertex pairs with multiple transported ports but a
perfectly flat **bit**: it is not rejected. Bounds too small to establish the
contract give an inconclusive result; no statuses of earlier candidate
families are changed.

## Testing the reunion law without a global coordinate system

From a root with `k` reciprocal ports, exhaust the `N=k(k−1)` two-step
nonbacktracking choices. Let `c_y` be the multiplicity arriving at each
reachable endpoint. Two- and three-way endpoint comparisons measure the
moments; the individual addresses need not be printed. The nonnegative
defect `(Σc³−4Σc²+4N)/N = Σ c(c−2)²/N` vanishes exactly when each route
has one alternative. Counting pairs alone can fail **at every root**:
a 24-vertex degree-five Cayley graph on S4 and the folded five-cube both
give `Σc²=40`, but their third moments are 92 and 80. Even the full local
test cannot distinguish the unfolded bipartite five-cube from its folded
counterpart; an actual odd return, not a familiar local signal, matters.
The weaker condition “at least one alternative” admits a smaller odd
graph on ten vertices. These experiments supply comparable preparations,
reunion sensing, complete trials and reliable resets.

Another countercontrol holds even arbitrary passive click times fixed.
Attach a rook or Shrikhande fiber to the same rank-three diamond product.
From one common fiber slice, all the compared single-signal return/hit
records agree, even for prescribed moving portals. A reversible dirty-store
and return-channel intervention tests the three missing chords of a
four-site path: twelve of a thousand three-hop words give a four-clique in
one world, none in the other. Neither graph is an inferred physical space;
the new comparison requires addressable reciprocal ports and local storage.

## Hidden sheets: closed paths are a stronger test than copied names

Three independent, disconnected copies preserve every two-step reunion.
Merely connecting three hidden sheets while keeping all five local ports
is not equivalent. In an ordinary graph cover, if every base square closes,
the five lifted involutions commute. One connected orbit then has at most
32 states, not 48. More sharply, at least four of the forty base squares
have nontrivial sheet transport in a connected threefold cover. Each square
lies in three cubes with disjoint other-face sets, and five flat faces of a
cube force the sixth to be flat. A nonidentity permutation of three sheets
moves at least two. The sharp construction misses 64 of the 960 directed
two-step reunions (1/15); a cyclic three-sheet construction needs at least
1/10. An observer who identifies only the projected base vertices sees no
failure at all. This is a resource bound **if** generations are modeled by
that particular kind of cover, not an exclusion of three physical families.

An exchange path has its own labeling pitfall. Two hard-core occupations
on a three-arm tree have a six-state configuration ring; one lap exchanges
the ordered lift despite there being no loop in physical space. Two
ten-step routes can be matched in all visited unordered configurations and
all directed spatial bond counts, with different lifted permutations.
That distinguishes exchange from ordinary route imbalance. It does not
produce a particle-statistics sign: a separate spectator-dependent
configuration-edge phase can imitate the odd sector on **every** closed
path. The program, coherent preparation and recombination are extra resources.

No coordinate chart, Lorentz metric, gravitational equation, physical
flavor state or actual single event was inferred from these finite checks.

## How many independently countable return periods?

Keep the graph fixed. Make its forty elementary squares return the same
record by default; then waive that requirement on a chosen subset. Count
closed paths up to the remaining square relations and **also** commute the
records of successive loops. In this explicitly abelian experiment, fewer
than nine waived faces never yield three independent integer periods. The
exact anchored search at nine finds 400 minimal sets in two symmetry
classes. In 160, three chosen edges at one root select their incident faces:
the group of return records is `Z³ × Z₂`. The other 240 have no common vertex
and yield `Z³`, without the torsion record. Other selections of **nine**
faces still have rank zero. Counting released conditions alone is not a
dimension measurement.

Waiving conditions along two marked directions instead of three gives
`Z² × Z₂`. For explicit rooted loops, counters taken modulo two and three
give respectively 16 and 54 distinguishable records in the rank-three
example, versus 8 and 18 in the rank-two example. Counting requires a
repeatable root, retained path records and the intervention which changes
the square conditions. It does not reconstruct an infinite space from a
finite observation, establish isotropy, or choose the release rule.
