# When can a packet move without a list of addresses?

The earlier localized packet admits several moves from the same singleton. Here the question is stricter: **is the next raw move uniquely selected by the present state?** Three candidates separate a supplied direction memory, an intrinsically directed shape, and conservation of its marks. None selects physical space or a unit of time.

### Same three links, two possible directions

Take the square of a cycle: along the underlying ring each site is linked to sites one or two steps away. For the anonymous shell table 16, prepare the three marked bonds {i−2,i}, {i−1,i}, {i−1,i+1}. Exactly two vertices can fire. Either move translates this entire shape by one site, in opposite directions. The full raw orbit on each of seven checked ring sizes is the cycle of these shapes, not just a sequence chosen to translate. Reflection preserves the input yet interchanges the possible outputs. There cannot be a deterministic choice between them based solely on that input and respecting its symmetry.

Remembering the last event and forbidding its immediate inverse gives persistent travel after one initial choice. This is one additional memory law. More generally, for involutive single-center events the event just taken can be undone: if the next event is *always unique*, the orbit under that restriction can have period at most two. To get longer directed motion one can supply memory or change the event law. A second packet also distinguishes otherwise identical rules: 128 tables agree on the isolated three-link gait, but on the tested two-packet sector only 64 still agree with rule 16.

### Directed motion from a binary shape, without a stored last event

On the same carrier with n ≥ 7, the two kinds of bonds are distinguished by incidence, without naming axes: the ends of a short bond have two common neighbors, the ends of a long bond one. This **uses the unmarked carrier** as well as the marks.

- **Copy/erase.** A short and a long mark meeting at one end form an asymmetric two-mark seed. A local copy at its free short tip creates an overlapping copy farther along; a local erasure removes the trailing pair. Reading the actual state is enough to distinguish the two phases. In eight fixtures, across all 468 phase/orientation states, exactly one event is enabled. Two events translate the seed by one site. The number of marks alternates between two and four.
- **Conserved pair.** Keep exactly one short and one long mark. When they touch, pivot the short bond away from the long bond's midpoint. If they are disjoint but exactly one long-bond endpoint is one short step from the short mark, pivot the long bond towards it. Exclude occupied destinations. Both the pivot and the destination come from the local incidence. With the chosen seed, three events translate the pair by two sites. Exhausting all **32,202** two-mark states on eleven carriers gives **1,692** moving states, exactly 6n per ring; all other two-mark states are idle. The moving cycles have length 3n/gcd(n,2). The reflected seed goes the other way. No step reads a preceding event, an address word, or a phase counter.

These are *new* directed local rules, not consequences of shell flag 16. The clean two-mark evolution is a permutation (idle states can be left fixed), not a global uniqueness result. Two preparations on a 16-site ring can give two different successors **at the same firing center**. Reporting only that center would hide the choice. The finite substrate and prepared shape are resources, not evidence of a naturally occurring particle.

### What the word “reversible” can hide

On a moving conserved-pair orbit let T be its unique successor. An incidence-only reversal J fixes the long bond and its midpoint, reverses the arm of the short bond and interchanges its separated and inner-touch phases. The outer-touch phase is preserved. On 414 raw states, J²=1 and JTJ=T⁻¹. A stationary observer asking “is the midpoint a long neighbor of my site?” sees even-event conditional returns **1, 1/3, 1/6, 1/2**. The rise is real and entirely classical. The full one-event kernel does *not* satisfy ordinary detailed balance; the [no-revival test](WHEN_SHUTTERS_AND_CLOCKS_CHANGE_THE_TEST.md) is not contradicted.

Choose instead the anonymous outer-touch phase as a yes/no reading. Fitting a two-state Markov process to its exact one-step transitions would give a two-step return of 1/2; the native return is zero. This is a lost phase of the packet, not evidence for a quantum state. Another reader can see an arrow of time: for three consecutive pivot centers (a,b,c), “b,c form a long bond and a is their unique common neighbor” occurs one-third of the time on the uniform active sector; its reverse never occurs. State reversal is not reversal of every instrument record.

Over any single shared history of passive binary values Q₀,Q₁,Q₂, the number Q₀Q₁+Q₁Q₂−Q₀Q₂ is either 1 or −3. Thus its mean K cannot exceed 1 with nonnegative weights. Across 545 tested native reader/interval choices the observed K range was −5/3 to 1. Separately *supply* correlations (1/2, 1/2, −1/2), so K=3/2. To encode these with signed weights on common histories costs at least **1/8 negative mass**; weights 3/8 on each of the three K=1 sign-product rows and −1/8 on the fourth attain it. Alternatively a mixture of passive histories and unrestricted pair contexts needs at least **1/4 contextual fraction**, also attainable. These are costs of the stipulated target, not negative detection probabilities or measured invasive fractions. The [existing temporal gate](../sections/171-qm-experiment-language-ledger.md) tests supplied correlators; this comparison computes actual native histories first.

For physical context [Robens et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3912) reported K=1.435 ± 0.074 with trapped atoms and an ideal-negative measurement protocol. The three correlations are obtained in the experiment's measurement configurations, not by logging all three binary values on the same undisturbed run. [Belle's neutral-meson study, Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0702267#S0.T1) gives a corrected opposite-minus-same flavor asymmetry of −0.974 ± 0.080 at a decay-time difference of 6–7 ps. It is a correlation of *two tagged decays*, with background, wrong-tag and deconvolution corrections, not the packet's passive same-event return. Neither publication fixes the missing physical reader or establishes the supplied equal-angle target for these graphs.
