# Two ways to test a clock

These two tests count different things. The first counts pump periods between detections in a published apparatus. The second counts repeated commands *within one freshly prepared circuit*. Its probabilities are predictions, not additional measured bins.

**Extend the earlier HBT control without changing its rule.** In [the first comparison](ONE_EVENT_NEEDS_MORE_THAN_A_SQUARE.md), a seven-phase deterministic word reproduced flat cross-arm side rates at lags ±3, ±4 and ±5. At lag seven it necessarily predicts no unlike arms. Summing the same ±1.5 ns windows in the [same source histogram](../data/pump_hbt/README.md) gives **64,825 and 65,016** counts at −7 and +7. Every signed side peak from one through nine periods is present. Thus every clean, static, exclusive-arm clock of period 1 through 9, advanced once per pump, has a forbidden peak in the measured delay range. These are retrospective additions to a public histogram, not independently recorded holdout data. Erasing correctly indexed detections does not generate an unlike pair, but phase slips, nonexclusive emission, false tags or a clock advancing only upon actual emission change the comparison.

The [extended replay](probe_pump_clock_lag_extension.py) constructs a balanced deterministic 1,024-phase binary cycle giving exactly equal frequencies to all four ordered pairs at each nonzero lag through nine. This control matches a pair-moment pattern, not every noisy bin. It predicts a missing opposite-arm peak at 1,024 periods, outside this file. A different null assumes a common stationary scalar intensity and that both detector responses are nondecreasing functions of it, with conditional factorization of the two detections. Pairing identical intensities maximizes their expected response product; the central coincidence is then at least the shifted one. In the archived windows the central count divided by the mean over all 18 side peaks is 0.01584. Applying the population inequality to those counts requires comparable exposure and temporal response, a shared intensity, and absence or a bound on acquisition artifacts. The pairs are not assumed independent. An exclusive single-carrier source or different, correlated detector dynamics is not in that null class.

**Five circuit depths instead of a long run.** Two overlapping trivalent rational contacts, repeated n times, are compared to a retained reference in a four-path interferometer. Launch and recombination use the same equal four-port junction. Reading the total squared signal in its designated output branch gives (1+x₃(n))/2 for the stated preparation. The [five-depth probe](probe_five_depth_memory_contrast.py) calculates this by operating on all sixteen modes. It compares it to any model with three complete hidden states, one fixed stochastic transition per two-contact block, and a fixed preparation and binary response. Differences of five successive endpoint probabilities would have to satisfy a recurrence whose trace coefficient is at least −1 for that stochastic model. The ideal signal requires −14/9. Bounding measurement discrepancies in the recurrence gives a *uniform* error lower bound of 10/207 on depths 0–4; a separately specified symmetric reference gives 40/297. These are finite robust design thresholds, not measured discrepancies or a new general result about infinite sequences.

Three states can fit the first four default endpoints exactly, and four states fit all five. A time-dependent controller or a larger hidden clock also evades the three-state contract. Counting four visible ports is not an independent bound on hidden memory. No number of states, detector law or Markovianity has been established for nature.
