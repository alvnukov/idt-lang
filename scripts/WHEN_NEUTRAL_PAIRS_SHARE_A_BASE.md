# When neutral pairs share a base

Two packets can project to the same pair of base vertices and still differ in a way the projection cannot recover: do they occupy the same sheet of the double cover or opposite sheets? The [neutral-pair probe](probe_neutral_pair_lift.py) keeps the distinction explicit. In the chart supplied by the marked base and chosen root sheet, 62 of 528 unordered packet pairs (repetitions allowed) have total charge label `q = 0`. Sixty are on opposite sheets and have total `X = 0`. The exceptions are `(0,0)` with `X = +6` and `(31,31)` with `X = −6`. Both exceptions and the opposite-sheet pair `(0,31)` project to the same base root pair. Swapping both sheets together preserves the same/opposite distinction; independent swaps erase it.

A packet with coordinate 0 is not the vacuum. The true empty configuration is `()`, with no packets at all. If a reversible source is **supplied**, it can interchange the empty state with `(0,31)` without choosing a sheet. Interchanging it with `(0,0)` or `(31,31)` instead requires an oriented reference for a deterministic, sheet-equivariant choice. Leaving the empty state unchanged is also equivariant. The supplied collision menu preserves `q`, and its chosen ternary move changes total `X` by ±12, but it does not create packets. Reading `X` as `3(B−L)` (three times the difference between baryon and lepton numbers) uses an external occupation dictionary; it does not identify any of these controls with a physical source.

## Can binary contacts implement the collision menu?

The [encoding probe](probe_binary_charge_encoding.py) checks the raw binary rule's invariant: the count of high sites modulo three. With one bit per logical slot, none of the 32,768 addressed polarity choices preserves that invariant for the full supplied menu. This excludes that direct encoding, not all binary implementations. With **two rails per slot**, one initially high, externally scheduled contacts reproduce all 21 specified endpoint maps. The ternary map alone uses 44 sites and 2,646 offers, with prepared helpers. Intermediate states can leave the encoding, and this word has no static additive real-valued charge matching `q` on encoded inputs and conserved at every firing. Correct endpoints do not supply a firing law or show that a microscopic charge persists.

The [pair tests](../tests/test_neutral_pair_lift.py) check the census after renaming base vertices and replay the source controls on the empty state, the two root-sheet singleton controls and every pair. The [encoding tests](../tests/test_binary_charge_encoding.py) check each channel on all its logical inputs and include a wrong-helper control. Neither probe predicts a decay rate or selects an interaction.

## What do the decay searches constrain?

For neutrinoless double beta decay, [KamLAND-Zen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11438) gives a combined lower half-life limit of 3.8 × 10²⁶ years for xenon-136 and [GERDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06079) gives 1.8 × 10²⁶ years for germanium-76. For the distinct neutrinoless *quadruple* beta decay channel in xenon-136, [XMASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05231) gives a lower half-life limit of 3.7 × 10²⁴ years. All three are 90% confidence limits for the named physical channels, not observed decays. Without a map from a packet process to a nuclear transition and its rate, none chooses a sheet relation or a vacuum source.
